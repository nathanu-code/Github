# Jullien Stellar — Five Meta Feed Ads

**Production sheet** · 4:5 · 1080×1350 · Meta + Instagram feed

Brand: near-black `#191219` / plum `#4A2B3C` (ground) · cream `#F6F1EA` (field) · rose gold `#B5836E`, tint `#D9AE99`, blush `#EFDCD3` (accent)
Type: **Marcellus** display (fallback Georgia) · **Jost** body (fallback Century Gothic / Futura) · **Space Mono** for labels, prices and the policy board only

These five boards adapt the *structure* of five ad concepts from a reference reel (@nawazmaqsood97, originally executed for a Nordic balm brand). The layouts, hooks and proof devices are the transferable part. Every word, claim, price and testimonial on these boards is Jullien's own — nothing was carried over from the reference brand.

## 1 · Board 01 — System / Hook

Deep plum ground, warm radial glow behind the device, four review bubbles fanned across the lower third.

> **Eyebrow** — READY TO DISCOVER WHAT SEOUL ALREADY KNOWS?
> **Headline** — The Complete Touchless System for Mature Skin
> **Bubbles** — "It gives my skin the most beautiful glow." *Sarah B.* · "Instantly more hydrated, balanced, refreshed." *Kelsee F.* · "The absorption is unlike anything I've used." *Debbie K.* · "My skin looks better than it did ten years ago." *Ryan C.*
> **Close** — VISIBLE REFINEMENT IN ONE WEEK
> **Footer** — ENGINEERED IN SEOUL · JULLIENSTELLAR.COM

**Changed from the reference, deliberately.** The reference stacks four unattributed DM bubbles beside stock-photo avatars — invented people saying invented things. Every bubble here is a real review from jullienstellar.com with the name the site publishes, and the avatars are gone rather than faked. It costs a little visual warmth and buys you an ad you can actually defend.

## 2 · Board 02 — Our Solutions

Cream field. Four concern → product rows, alternating side, warm-grey bubble for the concern and rose for the reply.

| Concern (customer voice) | Answer |
| :-- | :-- |
| everything I put on just sits on top of my skin | The Stellar Airbrush · $249 |
| dry and tight again by noon?? | Core Complex · from $250 |
| fine lines are setting in around my eyes | Stem Cell Complex · from $250 |
| honestly just tell me what to buy | The Essentials Set · $424.15 (was $499) |

**Four rows, not the reference's five.** Jullien has four purchasable lines. A fifth row means either inventing a SKU or pointing two concerns at one product, and the second reads as padding at scrolling speed. If the catalogue grows, the row rhythm takes a fifth without a redesign.

## 3 · Board 03 — Testimonial + Proof

Light gradient. Real quote as the headline, before/after pair, three proof points, device at right.

> **Headline** — "My skin looks better than it did ten years ago."
> **Attribution** — Ryan C. · verified customer
> **Proof** — 8% stem cell conditioned media — above the 5% standard in comparable formulas · Touchless micro-clustering atomization — no needles, no puncture, no downtime · Visible refinement in one week. Transformation in four.

**The before/after frames ship empty on purpose.** See §5 — this is the one board that cannot go live as drawn.

## 4 · Board 04 — Return Policy

White. Plum display headline, mono body with blush highlights, rose "SAVE 15%" seal, device and ampoule at right.

> **Headline** — Jullien's Return Policy? / Straight answers, no asterisks.
> **Body** — Your device is covered. If the Stellar Airbrush malfunctions, **we replace it.** / Ampoules are sealed actives, so they are **final sale** — the same reason they stay sterile. / Start with the set. It is the least expensive way to find out whether touchless works for your skin.
> **Seal** — THE ESSENTIALS SET · SAVE 15%

**This board does not copy the reference's promise.** The reference ad reads "You Won't Need It. But You're Covered with a 60-Day Guarantee. Better skin or your money back." Jullien's actual posted policy is replacement on device malfunction and non-refundable ampoules. Running the reference copy would be a straightforward false advertising claim, so the board states the real terms and turns the honesty itself into the angle.

The 15% is real arithmetic, not a round number chosen for the badge: $424.15 ÷ $499.00 = 0.85 exactly. If the set price moves, the seal has to move with it.

**If you decide to institute a real guarantee**, the reference's structure is worth having and this copy drops straight into the same layout:

> Jullien's Return Policy? / You won't need it. / But you're covered for **60 days**. Better skin, or your money back — device *and* ampoules.

Do not run that until the policy page, the checkout terms and the support team all say the same thing. A guarantee that exists only in the ad is the version regulators care about.

## 5 · Board 05 — Women Over 40

White. Oversized headline, single review in a cream field, the four-product lineup under a labelled rule.

> **Eyebrow** — ATTENTION
> **Headline** — WOMEN OVER 40
> **Review** — ★★★★★ "I've tried everything… I actually noticed a difference under my eyes. That hasn't happened before." — A.C. · Verified customer · Core Complex
> **Lineup** — THE COMPLETE RITUAL — from $249

**Changed from the reference, deliberately.** The reference renders its testimonial as a screenshotted Facebook comment complete with a reaction count. Dressing a review in another platform's chrome asserts it was posted there and drew that engagement; if it wasn't, that's a fabricated endorsement no matter how real the underlying quote is. This is a plain review card with the same words. The reference's emoji is also gone — Jullien's identity has no emoji in it.

## 6 · Assets to swap

Every product on these boards is **vector artwork drawn for this set**, not photography. It is deliberately stylised and holds up at feed size, but photography will outperform it on a cold audience. Swap in this order:

| Slot | Boards | What to shoot |
| :-- | :-: | :-- |
| **Before / after pair** | 03 | Real customer, unretouched, same lens / distance / light / expression on both frames, signed release naming the ad use. Highest priority and the only true blocker. |
| Airbrush in hand | 01, 03, 04 | Device held at ~45°, mist catching a rim light against the plum ground. The reference's power is the hand — it gives scale and makes the device feel real. |
| Ampoule cluster | 02, 05 | Three ampoules, one open, on cream. Shallow depth of field. |
| Set, styled | 02, 05 | Full set as shipped, overhead on cream. |

Keep the drawn ampoule and device as fallbacks: they are consistent across all five boards, which product photography from different sessions usually is not.

## 7 · Before you publish: the claims

These are the load-bearing claims. Ad claims are substantiable claims, and testimonial rules got sharper — the FTC's 2024 Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials makes fake or misattributed reviews directly penalisable, and the Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) require that a depicted result be one consumers can *generally* expect.

- **The five testimonials** (Sarah B., Kelsee F., Debbie K., Ryan C., A.C.) are transcribed from jullienstellar.com. Two things before they run: check each one **verbatim** against the live page, since I shortened two for the bubble layout and a trimmed quote must not change its meaning; and confirm you hold the customer's permission to use their words and initials in **paid** media. Site permission is not automatically ad permission.
- **A.C.'s quote appears on the site in two overlapping forms** — one mentioning the Core Complex and two weeks, one not. Board 05 uses the shorter form and puts "Core Complex" in the attribution line rather than inside the quotation marks. Keep that split. Merging them into one longer sentence would be writing a review the customer didn't write.
- **"8% stem cell conditioned media — above the 5% standard in comparable formulas"** is a *comparative* claim. You need a dated file showing which competitor formulas you surveyed and their disclosed concentrations. "Standard" is the word doing the work and it's the word that will be challenged.
- **"Visible refinement in one week. Transformation in four."** is a results claim on a timeline. Back it with a consumer-perception study or a documented user panel, and keep the substantiation on the landing page. If all you have is testimonials, this line has to soften.
- **"Transformation"** is the riskiest single word in the set. Cosmetics may claim to affect appearance; claims to change the structure or function of skin are drug claims under FDA rules, and "stem cell" language pulls a reviewer's eye straight to that line. It is defensible as written because it sits next to "visible refinement" and reads as appearance. Do not let it drift into "rebuilds", "regenerates" or "boosts collagen by X%" — that is a different regulatory category.
- **"Absorption comparable to microneedling"** appears on the website and is deliberately **not** on any board. It is a comparative efficacy claim against a clinical procedure and it would need penetration data. The boards say "no needles, no puncture, no downtime" instead — a description of what the device does, which needs no study.

### Meta policy, specifically

- **Before/after is no longer an automatic rejection.** Meta moved its health and wellness rules to a claims-based standard on 22 July 2026; side-by-side imagery in cosmetic ads now fails only when it carries a violative claim or shows unexpected or unlikely results. Board 03 is therefore runnable — but the pair you drop in must be a *typical* outcome, not your single best responder.
- **Board 05's headline is the one to watch.** Meta's personal-attributes standard bars ads that imply the advertiser knows a viewer's age. Their own examples: "Meet seniors" passes, "Meet other seniors" fails, "Ready to upgrade your skin to look younger?" fails. "WOMEN OVER 40" uses no second person and no "other", so it sits on the permitted side of that line — but it is a direct age callout and reviewers are inconsistent. If it gets knocked back, **"Made for skin over 40"** or **"Formulated for mature skin"** describes the product instead of the viewer and keeps the layout.
- Nothing in the set uses "you" attached to an age or a skin condition. Preserve that when you rewrite hooks — it is the single most common rejection in this category.

## 8 · A/B test plan

Change one thing per test. Board 01 is the cheapest place to learn, because only the eyebrow and headline move.

| | Eyebrow | Headline | Tests |
| :-: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Control | Ready to discover what Seoul already knows? | The Complete Touchless System for Mature Skin | Curiosity + provenance |
| A | Engineered in Seoul. Measured in results. | The Complete Touchless System for Mature Skin | Credentials over curiosity |
| B | No needles. No downtime. No hands. | The Complete Touchless System for Mature Skin | Mechanism as the hook |
| C | Ready to discover what Seoul already knows? | Everything You Apply Is Sitting On Top of Your Skin | Problem-first |
| D | 8% stem cell conditioned media | The Standard Is 5% | Specificity, number-led |

Across boards, the test that usually matters more than any headline is **01 vs 05 on cold traffic** — luxury system framing against blunt audience callout. They fail differently and one of them will be materially cheaper for you.

Judge on 3-second hold rate first, then outbound CTR, then cost per add-to-cart. One week per variant minimum, or run them in parallel in separate ad sets. Don't read a winner off a day.

## 9 · Export

Each artboard exports as a PNG from the canvas toolbar. 1080×1350 is the 4:5 feed size and is the only one you need for feed and IG; Meta down-samples for every other placement.

For Stories and Reels, don't crop these — 4:5 into 9:16 either letterboxes or eats the close. Rebuild as 1080×1920 with the headline in the upper third and everything below y=1600 kept clear of the UI (bottom 320px, right 180px).

Two caveats on export: PNG and PDF export render the fallback faces, not Marcellus and Jost, so proof the exports rather than the canvas before handing files to a buyer. And the drawn product art is SVG — it scales cleanly to any size you need for other placements.
