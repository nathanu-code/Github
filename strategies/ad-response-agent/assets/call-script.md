# Call script

`leadgap.py script <id>` prints this with the prospect's details filled in. This
file explains *why* each beat is shaped the way it is, so you can improvise
without losing the mechanism.

**The governing rule:** you are reporting a finding, not pitching a product. Do
not say "AI" in the first thirty seconds. The moment it becomes a pitch, you're
one of the hundred thousand people who watched the same reel.

---

## Beat 1 — The opener

> "Hi, is this the owner? Great — my name's ___. I'll be quick and this isn't
> really a sales call yet. I saw you're running seven ads for med spa services in
> Albuquerque, so I filled out your form to see what happens on the other side.
> Nobody got back to me for two days. I wanted to check whether that's normal or
> whether I just caught you on a bad day."

Why it works:

- **"Isn't really a sales call yet"** is true and it buys you the next fifteen
  seconds. "Yet" keeps you honest.
- **The ad count** proves you did specific homework on *them*, which almost no
  cold caller does.
- **"I filled out your form"** is the whole strategy in five words. It is
  concrete, verifiable, and slightly alarming.
- **"Or whether I just caught you on a bad day"** hands them an exit. It converts
  an accusation into a question, which is why they answer instead of hanging up.

## Beat 2 — Then shut up

Silence here is doing work. They will land in one of three places:

| They say | What it means | Your move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "That shouldn't have happened." | They know. It's a sore spot. | Go straight to the bridge. |
| "We get back to everyone." | They believe their process works. | "That's what I figured — that's why I called instead of assuming. Mine came in Tuesday at 8:34 in the morning, if it helps to look." |
| "Sarah must have missed it." | They're blaming staff. | "Sure — and that's kind of the point. It shouldn't depend on Sarah being at her desk." |

Do not fill the pause. The first person to talk after the finding loses the
frame.

## Beat 3 — The bridge

> "Here's why I called: you're already paying for those clicks. The leads are
> showing up. The only thing broken is the first ninety seconds. I put in an ad
> response agent that texts them back in about five seconds and books the ones
> worth booking. It doesn't touch anything else you've got running."

Four things are happening in that paragraph:

1. **"Already paying"** — reframes this as recovering spend, not new spend.
2. **"The only thing broken"** — shrinks the problem to something buyable today.
3. **"Texts them back"** — text, not voice, is the honest lead with the highest
   answer rate and the fastest install. Voice is an upsell.
4. **"Doesn't touch anything else"** — kills the integration fear before they
   raise it, which is the objection that quietly kills most of these deals.

## Objection handling

### "We're not interested."

**Source version** (from the reel — high risk, use knowingly):

> "So you're not interested in making more money automatically, on autopilot, for
> your business whatsoever? Help me understand that."

It's a loaded question with an implied insult. With an owner who has been sold to
before, it ends the call. The salvageable part is *help me understand* — the rest
is a strawman.

**Recommended version:**

> "Fair enough. Can I ask one thing before I go — is it that the timing's bad, or
> that you don't think the missed ones are worth chasing? I ask because I've got
> the timestamp on mine and I'd rather be wrong out loud."

Same reframe, no trap. "Before I go" removes the pressure that triggers the
brush-off, the either/or makes answering easier than hanging up, and "I'd rather
be wrong out loud" invites them to correct you — which is a conversation.

### "We already have someone who handles that."

> "Totally — I'm not trying to replace them. They just weren't there at 8:34 on
> Friday morning. Nobody can be. That's the only gap I'm filling."

Never position against their staff. You will lose, and you should.

### "Is this AI? We tried an AI thing and it was terrible."

> "Yeah, most of them are. The ones that fail try to be a receptionist — handle
> everything, sound human, run the whole conversation. This one does one thing:
> new lead comes in, it texts them inside five seconds, asks two questions, and
> hands off to a person. If it can't answer something it says so and gets you."

Agreeing with the objection is what makes the distinction land.

### "How much?"

Do not quote on this call.

> "Depends on volume — how many form fills or calls a week are you getting off
> those ads right now?"

That question prices the deal and qualifies them in one move. If they don't know
the number, that is itself the finding: they're buying traffic they don't count.

### "Send me some information."

Usually a soft no. Make it cost something:

> "Happy to — I'll send the audit with the timestamps. One thing so I don't waste
> your time: if the numbers hold up, is this something you'd actually want fixed
> this quarter, or is it a next-year thing?"

### "How did you get my number?"

> "Your Facebook ad, same as a customer would. That's the whole reason I called —
> I came in through your front door and nobody was home."

Straight answer, and it re-lands the finding.

## The close

> "Let me send you the two-minute version of what I found — timestamps and all.
> If it looks off, tell me and I'll drop it. What's the best email?"

"If it looks off, tell me and I'll drop it" is the strongest line in the script.
It's a real offer, it signals you have nothing to hide, and it makes accepting the
email cost them nothing.

Send the audit (`leadgap.py report <id>`) within the hour — while you are still
demonstrating, in person, the exact behaviour you're selling them.

---

## Voicemail (you will leave many)

Keep it under twenty seconds. One finding, no pitch:

> "Hi, this is ___. I filled out the contact form on your site Tuesday morning
> and never heard back — I think something's broken in your lead routing and I
> wanted to flag it. No pitch, just thought you'd want to know. ___-___-____."

Then send the audit by email the same day, referencing the voicemail.
