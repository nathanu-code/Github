# Surplus Disposition

Decides **what to do with an item and what to charge** for the Waipahu surplus
operation. `craigslist-listings` writes the listing once that decision is made.

## When to Activate

- New surplus, overstock, returns, or a liquidation lot arrives
- Deciding whether to hold inventory or move it
- Pricing a surplus item
- Stock is aging and not moving
- Working through equipment liquidation
- Choosing a sales channel for a given item

## The core principle

**Surplus inventory is a depreciating asset occupying a finite resource.**

Warehouse space in Waipahu has a cost, and every square foot holding a slow item
is unavailable to a fast one. The instinct to hold out for a better price is
usually wrong, because the carrying cost is invisible while the forgone price is
visible.

The right question is never "what is this worth?" It is **"what will this clear
at, this month, and is that better than the space?"**

## Disposition decision

Run in order. Stop at the first that fits.

**1. Can it be used in a project?**
If it matches something in the Grandeur or Ke'a pipeline, that is the highest
recovery — full retail value, no listing effort, no aging. Check live quotes
before listing anything as surplus. This is the most commonly missed option.

**2. Is it new, current, and desirable?**
List retail through the surplus channel at a real discount to new. Highest
recovery of the liquidation options, slowest to clear.

**3. Is it saleable but slow, or a large quantity?**
Bundle or wholesale. A lot sale to a contractor or another dealer at a lower
unit price often nets more than piecing out over months, once carrying cost and
listing labor are counted.

**4. Is it damaged, obsolete, or unsaleable at any reasonable price?**
Donate for the write-off where eligible, or scrap. **Decide this quickly.**
Damaged goods do not improve in storage, and the space they occupy is the real
cost.

## Pricing

Anchor on **what the buyer can verify**, not on what we paid. Sunk cost is not a
pricing input. A buyer comparing against retail does not know or care about our
landed cost.

Rough recovery bands against current retail:

| Condition | Typical band |
| --- | --- |
| New, in box, current model | 50–70% |
| New, discontinued or dated | 35–50% |
| Open box / display, excellent | 30–45% |
| Used, good working order | 25–40% |
| Damaged but functional | 10–25% |
| Parts / scrap value | Nominal |

These are starting points, not rules. Actual clearing price depends on how thin
the Oahu market is for that specific item — and it is thin for most things.
A narrow buyer pool means slower clearing and a lower realistic band than a
Mainland comparison would suggest.

**Equipment is different.** CNC routers, panel saws, compressors, forklifts, and
spray systems have a small, knowledgeable buyer pool. Price against actual
comparable sold listings, and verify make and model against records before
pricing — `craigslist-listings` documents a case where a compressor was
mislabeled by brand, which would have mispriced it in both directions.

## The aging ladder

Set the price to clear on a schedule, and decide the whole ladder **when you
list**, not when it stalls. Deciding a markdown while staring at an unsold item
invites holding out.

```
Day 0      List at target price
Day 30     No serious inquiries → reduce ~15%
Day 60     Still holding → reduce to wholesale/lot territory
Day 90     Bundle it, wholesale it, donate it, or scrap it — but move it
```

Ninety days is a decision point, not a suggestion. An item that has not sold in
three months at declining prices is not mispriced by a little — it is either
priced wrong by a lot or has no local buyer. Both conclusions point the same way.

should be replaced with intervals derived from your own data.

## Channel selection

| Channel | Best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Craigslist | Equipment, materials, bulk lots | Individual-seller tone required; one listing at a time, spaced 2–3 days — see `craigslist-listings` |
| Facebook Groups | Cabinets, appliances, consumer items | Marketplace account suspended, appeal exhausted — target Groups |
| Contractor / trade direct | Lots, wholesale quantities | Fastest clearing, lowest unit price, no listing labor |
| Retail pipeline | Anything matching a live quote | Highest recovery — always check first |
| Donation | Damaged or obsolete but usable | Write-off value; confirm eligibility and documentation |
| Scrap | No saleable value | Recover the space |

## Working a liquidation lot

When a full shop or estate comes in:

1. **Inventory before pricing.** Photograph everything, capture make and model
   from nameplates, and note condition. Records first, decisions second.
2. **Triage into the four dispositions** above before writing any listing.
   Handling items twice is the main labor cost.
3. **Identify the anchors.** In any lot, a small number of items carry most of
   the value. Price and list those carefully and first; they fund the effort.
4. **Bundle the tail.** Low-value items individually listed consume more labor
   than they return. Group them into lots.
5. **Sequence the listings.** Bulk posting triggers Craigslist removal — space
   them 2–3 days. Build the masterlist up front and work it down over weeks; a
   .docx masterlist by category is the established format.
6. **Set the aging ladder at listing time**, per item.

## Honesty in condition

State condition accurately, including defects. The surplus channel runs on
in-person pickup and repeat contractor buyers — a buyer who drives to Waipahu and
finds the item worse than described does not come back, and the local trade
community is small enough that this compounds.

Accurate condition disclosure also prevents the return that costs more than the
sale earned.

## Checklist

- [ ] Checked against live retail quotes before treating as surplus
- [ ] Make and model verified against records, not assumed
- [ ] Photographed before listing
- [ ] Disposition chosen deliberately, not defaulted to "hold"
- [ ] Priced against comparable sold listings, not against what we paid
- [ ] Aging ladder set at listing time
- [ ] Channel matched to item type
- [ ] Condition stated accurately, defects included
