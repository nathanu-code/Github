# Reselling Alibaba Goods — The Margin Math

Captured 2026-09-08. Sources: [Ropes & Gray — SCOTUS strikes down IEEPA tariffs](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/02/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-key-takeaways-and-implications-for-importers),
[K&L Gates — summary of the decision](https://www.klgates.com/Summary-Supreme-Court-Decision-on-IEEPA-Tariffs-2-20-2026),
[Congressional Research Service LSB11398](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11398),
[Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, No. 24-1287 (opinion PDF)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf),
[Ginger Control — import duty from China 2026](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/importing-from-china-tariff-guide)

A reusable framework for any product in this directory. **The headline: the FOB
price on Alibaba is usually 35–50% of your true landed-and-sold cost.** Quoting
margin off FOB is the single most common way these deals look good and aren't.

## ⚠ The tariff picture is genuinely unsettled right now

This changed materially in 2026 and is still moving. **Get a licensed customs
broker to classify your specific HTS code and quote the current stack in
writing before you commit capital.** Treat everything below as orientation, not
as a rate you can rely on.

**What happened.** On **20 February 2026** the Supreme Court decided
*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* (No. 24-1287), consolidated with
*Trump v. V.O.S. Selections*. By **6–3**, the Court held that **IEEPA does not
authorize the President to impose tariffs**, invalidating both the April 2025
"reciprocal" tariffs and the trafficking/fentanyl tariffs. The Court of
International Trade estimated the resulting refund exposure at
**$166–175 billion**.

**What replaced them.** The administration issued **Executive Order 14389**
ending those IEEPA actions and a new proclamation under **§122 of the Trade Act
of 1974** — which is **capped at 15% and expires after 150 days** unless
Congress extends it. CBP announced a **10%** rate across all countries under it.

**What did not change: Section 301.** The China-specific Section 301 tariffs
(**7.5–25%** for most goods) were never at issue and remain. Reporting also
describes a **12.5% Section 301 forced-labor baseline** taking over as the flat
§122 surcharge lapsed. Combined stacks for most consumer goods land in the
**~25–40%** range.

**De minimis is gone.** The $800 duty-free exemption no longer applies to
Chinese goods — **every shipment requires formal customs entry regardless of
value.** The "just order small parcels" strategy is dead.

Two consequences for you:

1. **Model 30–40% duty and sanity-check the downside at 40%.** If the deal only
   works at 10%, it isn't a deal.
2. **If you imported during the invalidated period, you may be owed refunds.**
   Talk to a broker about protective claims — there are deadlines.

## The full cost stack

| Layer | Typical | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **FOB price** | quoted | What the listing shows |
| **Ocean freight** | Varies wildly | **The margin killer on bulky goods.** LCL is priced per CBM |
| Destination/port charges, drayage | $200–600/shipment | Fixed — punishes small orders |
| **Customs duty** | **~25–40%** of customs value | Section 301 + MFN + current surcharge |
| Customs broker / entry fee | $100–250/entry | Per entry, now mandatory on everything |
| Inland freight to warehouse | Varies | |
| **Marketplace referral fee** | **15%** of sale price | Amazon standard |
| **Fulfillment (FBA)** | $20–40+ for large/bulky | Dimensional weight, not actual weight |
| Storage | Monthly, worse for bulky | Long-term storage fees escalate |
| **Advertising (PPC)** | **10–15%** of revenue | Higher in competitive categories |
| Returns / damage | 3–8% | Higher for fragile or complex items |
| Payment processing (direct sales) | ~3% | If selling off-marketplace |

## Worked example — a 4×4 complete grow tent kit

Using the mid-spec listings from the screenshots at roughly **$90 FOB**, against
Amazon complete-kit retail around **$350**.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | **$350.00** |
| FOB cost | −$90.00 |
| Ocean freight (~0.3 CBM @ LCL) | −$70.00 |
| Duty @ 30% of FOB | −$27.00 |
| **Landed cost** | **−$187.00** |
| Amazon referral (15%) | −$52.50 |
| FBA fulfillment (bulky) | −$35.00 |
| Storage (amortised) | −$5.00 |
| Returns allowance (5%) | −$17.50 |
| PPC (~13%) | −$45.00 |
| **Net per unit** | **≈ $8** |
| **Margin** | **≈ 2%** |

**Two percent.** One freight-rate increase, one PPC bidding war, or one bad
returns month puts that negative. And that assumes you can hold $350 retail
against incumbents.

### Selling direct instead

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $350.00 |
| Landed cost | −$187.00 |
| Payment processing (3%) | −$10.50 |
| Domestic shipping (bulky) | −$45.00 |
| Returns (5%) | −$17.50 |
| **Before customer acquisition** | **$90** |
| Customer acquisition cost | **−$50 to −$80** |
| **Net** | **≈ $10–40 (3–11%)** |

Better — but **customer acquisition is the whole business**, and see the
advertising problem below.

## Why grow tents specifically are a bad first product

1. **Bulk destroys the freight math.** You pay by volume. A folded tent plus
   light, fan, filter and ducting is ~0.3 CBM of mostly air, and it costs the
   same to ship whether it sells for $100 or $400.
2. **The category is brand-dominated.** VIVOSUN, Spider Farmer, Mars Hydro, and
   AC Infinity own the search results, buy at 500–5,000 unit tiers, and have
   review moats in the thousands. You'd enter at $90 FOB against competitors
   landing near $50–60.
3. **You cannot advertise normally.** Grow tents are cannabis-adjacent. Google
   and Meta restrict the ad categories, and marketplaces apply their own
   limits — so the cheap paid-acquisition channels are partly closed, which is
   exactly why CAC is the killer in the direct model above.
4. **It's a commodity.** Nothing in a grow tent is hard to make. There is no
   defensible spec, so competition is purely on price and reviews, and you have
   neither advantage.

## What actually carries margin

Invert every factor above:

| Want | Avoid |
|---|---|
| **High value per kilogram** | Bulky, low-value |
| Niche with no dominant brand | Brand-saturated categories |
| Technically complex enough to have a moat | Pure commodities |
| Advertisable through normal channels | Restricted categories |
| B2B buyers who spec rather than price-shop | Consumer price comparison |
| Simple, non-hazmat shipping | Lithium (UN38.3/Class 9 hazmat), pressure vessels |

## Rules of thumb

1. **Never quote margin off FOB.** Build the full stack every time.
2. **Model duty at 30–40%** and check survival at 40%.
3. **Value density is the first filter.** If the box is mostly air, stop.
4. **A 2× markup on landed cost is break-even on Amazon**, not profit. You need
   **3–4× landed** for a real margin after fees and ads.
5. **Order MOQ 1 first** where offered, and test before scaling. The
   [$292 koi filter](aquaponics.md) and the
   [$184 induction cooker](alibaba-yihouse-dc-induction-cooker.md) both allow
   this; the [$230 stove MOQ](alibaba-vooma-camping-stove.md) does not.
6. **Certification is a cost line, not a checkbox.** Gas, electrical, and
   battery products need real certification to sell legally in the US — budget
   it, and see [`sourcing-from-china.md`](sourcing-from-china.md).
7. **Get a customs broker before capital, not after.** HTS classification
   determines your duty rate, and a mixed kit can classify several different
   ways.
