# Solar & Thermal Distillation — No-Gear Desalination

## Why it works

Salt does not evaporate. Heat seawater and only the water leaves as vapour; the
salt stays behind as crust. Condense that vapour on a cool surface and you have
distilled water — in fact *purer* than RO output, because distillation leaves
behind not just salt but most pathogens and heavy metals too.

The physics is free and foolproof. The **throughput is the problem.**

## Two methods

### Solar still

Place a cup inside a wide bowl of seawater. Cover the bowl with clear plastic
wrap, weight the centre with a small stone to form an inverted cone, and set it
in full sun. Water evaporates, condenses on the underside of the plastic, runs
down the cone, and drips into the dry cup.

Needs: a container, clear plastic, a weight, and sun. No fuel, no noise, no
moving parts, nothing to break.

### Fire distillation

Boil seawater in a covered pot with the lid angled so condensate runs to one
edge, and channel the steam through a tube into a separate clean bottle. Salt
crust remains in the boiling pot.

Faster than solar and works at night or in overcast weather — but consumes fuel
continuously, and fuel is usually scarcer than sunlight.

## Yield reality check

This is the part most survival write-ups skip.

| Configuration | Realistic output |
|---|---|
| Ideal-condition lab still | 4–6 L per m² per day |
| Typical real-world still | 0.3–1.0 L per m² per day |
| 1–2 m² basin still, good sun | ~0.5–1.5 L per day |
| Improvised bowl-and-plastic still | **a few hundred mL per day at best** |

One adult needs roughly **3 L per day**. Supplying a small family needs a
purpose-built still around **5 m²** — a permanent installation, not something
improvised on a raft.

**So: the bowl-and-plastic-wrap still described in most survival guides will not
keep you alive.** It is a supplement that slows dehydration, not a water supply.
It matters because it costs nothing and needs no gear — never because it is
sufficient.

## Where it fits

- **Genuine use:** static coastal camping, a shore-based bug-out position, or
  supplementing a mechanical system. Anywhere you have area, sun, and time but
  not equipment.
- **Not a substitute** for an RO device in a raft or ditch bag. Area and sun
  are exactly what you do not have adrift.
- **Best role:** the fallback when gear fails. Everyone should know the method;
  nobody should plan around its numbers.

## Notes / due diligence

- Scale is everything: yield tracks **collection area**, so a bigger, shallower
  basin beats a deeper one. Maximise surface, not volume.
- Distilled water is mineral-free and flat-tasting. Fine short-term; over weeks
  it wants electrolyte supplementation.
- Do not let condensate contact the brine. A splash of seawater into the
  collection cup ruins the batch.
- Fire distillation's real cost is fuel. Budget it before relying on the method.
