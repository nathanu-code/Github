## What it is

The long-standing reference point for hand-powered marine desalination — issued
in life rafts, ditch bags, and military survival kits. A compact hand pump
forces seawater through a reverse osmosis membrane at pressure. No batteries,
no fuel.

Where QuenchSea competes on volume and price, the Survivor 06 competes on
ruggedness and track record.

## Trade-off vs. QuenchSea 3.0

| | Katadyn Survivor 06 | QuenchSea 3.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Output | 0.89 L/hr | 5–8 L/hr |
| Weight | 1.13 kg | 6 kg |
| Price | ~$1,000–$1,700 | $400 |
| Salt rejection | 98.4% avg | 98% |
| Track record | Decades, military-issue | Newer entrant |

The Survivor 06 is **5× lighter and ~7× lower output**. That is the whole
decision: it is a *personal* survival device sized for a ditch bag, not a
water supply for a crew. QuenchSea's 6 kg is a real penalty in a grab bag but
its throughput is in a different class.

## Practical notes

- 0.89 L/hr means **continuous pumping for an hour** to get under a litre. In a
  raft, in heat, on a calorie deficit, sustained pumping is the binding
  constraint — not the membrane.
- Rated for 1–6 people, but at 0.89 L/hr six people is a survival ration, not
  hydration. One person needs ~3 L/day; that is over 3 hours of pumping.
- The 5-year lifetime assumes proper storage and periodic membrane care.
  Verify service state before relying on a unit that has sat in a raft.

## Notes / due diligence

- Price spread across retailers is wide (~$1,000 to ~$1,700 for nominally the
  same family). Confirm the exact part number before comparing quotes.
- Cost per litre/hour of capacity is roughly **30–50× QuenchSea's**. Justified
  by weight and pedigree, not by throughput.
