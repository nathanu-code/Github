## What it is

A multi-chamber pouch with a semi-permeable hydrophilic membrane between the
chambers. You fill the outer chamber with raw seawater and inject a 4 oz sugary
"syrup charge" into the inner chamber. The concentrated syrup creates an osmotic
gradient that pulls water molecules across the membrane on its own — **no
pressure, no pumping, no power**. It takes roughly 5 hours per pouch.

The output is not pure water. It is a sweet, electrolyte-rich sports drink,
because the syrup you started with is still in there — now diluted by the water
it pulled in.

## How it differs from reverse osmosis

RO **pushes** water through a membrane against osmotic pressure using
mechanical force. FO lets osmosis run **in its natural direction** toward a
solution more concentrated than seawater. That is why it needs no pump — and
why it needs a consumable syrup charge for every use.

| | Reverse osmosis | Forward osmosis pouch |
|---|---|---|
| Driving force | Mechanical pressure (you) | Osmotic gradient (syrup) |
| Effort | Continuous hard pumping | None — wait ~5 hrs |
| Output | Fresh water | Sweet electrolyte drink |
| Limited by | Your stamina | Number of syrup charges |
| Speed | Minutes | ~5 hours |

## Performance

- **Salt removal:** ~97% (lower than RO's ~98%+).
- **HTI X-Pack:** rated up to 32 L of drinkable output over a 10-day period.
- **Per pouch:** one syrup charge yields roughly the pouch's fill volume of
  drink, then the pouch and charge are spent.

## Availability — read this first

**SeaPack is out of production and HTI (Hydration Technology Innovations) has
closed.** Retailers list it as no longer available. Any SeaPack stock found now
is old inventory with membranes of unknown age.

The commonly available substitute is the **OTS Osmo Seal Fresh & Salt Water
Filter**, sold through Amazon. Its published specifications and independent
testing are far thinner than HTI's were — treat performance claims as
unverified until you test a unit.

## Where it fits

This is a **backup to a backup**, not a primary system:

- **Strength:** works when you physically cannot pump — injury, exhaustion,
  hypothermia, or a casualty who needs water while you handle the boat. That is
  a genuine gap that no hand pump fills.
- **Weakness:** hard-capped by consumables. When the syrup charges are gone,
  the pouch is dead weight. An RO pump keeps working as long as you can move
  your arm.
- **The sugar:** an electrolyte drink is a real benefit for a dehydrated
  casualty. It is a liability if you need plain water for a wound, an infant,
  or a long ration plan.

## Notes / due diligence

- 5-hour latency means you must start it **before** you are desperate.
- Verify syrup-charge expiry. The charge is a food product with a shelf life
  shorter than the membrane's.
- Check pouch count against realistic days adrift. Four pouches is one day of
  hydration for one person, not a survival plan.
