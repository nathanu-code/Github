# Crop Selection: What's Actually Easiest to Grow

## The tension in one line

**The easiest things to grow have almost no calories. The calorie crops are
harder and want soil.** Every recommendation below sits somewhere on that
trade-off, so it's worth naming up front.

## Tier 1 — Easiest in aquaponics (low nutrient demand)

These are "the backbone of many aquaponic systems" — they grow fast, **tolerate
swings in water quality**, and give repeated harvests off one planting. That
water-quality tolerance is the real reason they're easy: a beginner's system
wobbles, and these forgive it.

| Crop | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Lettuce** | The easiest and fastest. ~28 days |
| **Watercress** | **The natural fit** — semi-aquatic, thrives in flowing water, very nutrient-dense |
| Chard | Cut-and-come-again for months from one plant |
| Kale, spinach, mustard greens | Repeat harvest, cold-tolerant |
| Arugula (salad rocket) | Very fast |
| Pak choi / bok choy | Fast, compact |
| **Basil** | Highest value per square foot; roots from cuttings |
| Mint, chives, oregano, thyme, sage | Perennial, propagate from cuttings |
| Parsley, cilantro | Reliable, repeat cutting |
| Scallions | Regrow from the base indefinitely |

**Best picks if you want the least trouble:** lettuce, basil, and watercress.
Watercress in particular is the crop aquaponics was practically designed for.

Herbs also solve the [seed problem](seeds-and-propagation.md) — they root from
cuttings, so one mother plant is a permanent supply.

## Tier 2 — Fruiting crops (high nutrient demand)

The botanical fruits — tomatoes, eggplant, cucumber, zucchini, strawberries,
peppers — all need **more nutrients, particularly potassium and phosphorus**,
than leafy greens. They are "more appropriate for established systems with
adequate fish stocks," which is the polite way of saying **don't try these in
your first season.**

| Crop | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Strawberries** | Easiest fruit | **One of the most popular aquaponics fruits** — grows well in both NFT and DWC. Propagates from runners, no seed needed |
| **Cucumbers** | Moderate | Vigorous and high-yielding, but need grow-bed depth and a trellis |
| Peppers | Moderate | Slower than tomatoes, less demanding |
| Zucchini / summer squash | Moderate | Enormous yields, but physically large plants |
| **Tomatoes** | Hardest of these | Highest yield potential, but need the most depth, structural support, and supplementation |
| Eggplant | Hardest | Same demands as tomatoes, slower |

**Start with strawberries.** They're the one fruiting crop that behaves like a
Tier 1 plant, they suit both common growing methods, and runners give you free
plants forever.

## The three nutrients your system will always lack

Aquaponic systems are "routinely deficient in a few nutrients — primarily
**potassium, calcium, and iron**." Fish feed simply doesn't supply enough of
them for fruiting crops.

**Iron is the one you'll actually see.** Here's the mechanism, and it's a trap
built into the system design:

- Aquaponics runs at **pH 6.8–7.2** — the compromise between what fish and
  bacteria want and what plants want.
- **Above pH 7.0, iron becomes chemically unavailable to plants.** Iron
  solubility wants pH 6.0–6.5.
- So the pH that keeps your fish healthy is the pH that starves your plants of
  iron.

**Diagnosis is unmistakable: leaves yellowing while the veins stay green.**
That's interveinal chlorosis, and it means iron. Fix it with **chelated iron**
(DTPA or EDDHA form — plain iron sulphate precipitates out at this pH).

Budget for chelated iron, potassium (kelp meal or potassium bicarbonate, which
also buffers), and calcium the moment you plant anything fruiting.

## Tier 3 — Don't bother in aquaponics

| Crop | Why not |
|---|---|
| Carrots, beets, turnips, parsnips | Need deep media; won't work in DWC or NFT at all |
| **Potatoes** | Need soil depth and a dry-down period |
| Corn | Wind-pollinated, tall, enormous nutrient demand |
| Melons, watermelon | Space consumption is absurd relative to yield |
| Cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower heads | Long season, heavy feeders, one harvest per plant |
| Onion / garlic bulbs | Long season, need to cure dry |
| **Blueberries** | **Impossible.** They need pH 4.5–5.5; your system runs 6.8–7.2 |

**One exception worth knowing: radishes.** Fast (~25 days) and shallow-rooted
enough to work in a media bed, unlike every other root crop.

## The calorie crops — and why they need soil

This is the honest counterpoint to everything above. Leafy greens are
essentially calorie-free. If food production rather than fresh produce is the
goal, the ranking looks completely different:

| Crop | Calories per sq ft | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Potatoes** | **70–180** (commonly 100–150) | **More than any other commonly grown vegetable** |
| **Sweet potatoes** | Comparable, better in zone 7+ | **Store nearly twice as long** as potatoes. Propagate from slips — no seed |
| Winter squash | High | Stores for months without processing |
| **Dry beans** | Lower per sq ft | But store far longer, and supply the **protein potatoes can't** |
| **Dent corn** (not sweet) | High | Grinds to meal, stores for years — but **demands the most space, so it's the first thing to cut** in a small plot |

The standard guidance is that **70–80% of growing space should go to
calorie-dense crops** for food production to contribute meaningfully.

**None of these work in aquaponics.** They want soil, depth, and a dry-down.

## So the right architecture is both

| System | Grows | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Aquaponics / hydro** | Greens, herbs, strawberries | Fresh vitamins, minerals, fast turnover, year-round |
| **Soil beds or grow bags** | Potatoes, sweet potatoes, beans, squash | **The calories** |
| **Sprouts in a jar** | Alfalfa, radish, broccoli, mung bean | [Fresh food with zero power](seeds-and-propagation.md) |

These aren't competing options. The aquaponics system covers exactly what stored
staples lack (fresh vitamins), and the soil beds cover exactly what aquaponics
can't produce (calories). Sprouts cover the case where the power is out.

**If you only do one thing: grow potatoes or sweet potatoes in grow bags.** No
system, no pumps, no power, highest calories per square foot of anything
available, and sweet potato slips mean no seed purchase ever.

## If you're in Hawaii, several answers change

Your other work suggests Waipahu — tell me if that's right, because a tropical
zone materially changes these recommendations, mostly in your favour:

1. **The tank-heating cost disappears.** I flagged heating as the reason most
   temperate aquaponics systems fail their first January. Tilapia want 25–28 °C;
   in Hawaii that's ambient year-round. **This removes the single largest
   operating cost from the [aquaponics record](aquaponics.md).**
2. **Sweet potatoes strongly outperform potatoes** — they beat white potatoes in
   zone 7 and warmer, and you're well past that.
3. **Taro (kalo) becomes available, and it's the best fit here** — a
   calorie-dense staple that grows in standing water, which is the one calorie
   crop that suits a wet system rather than fighting it.
4. **Year-round growing** removes the seasonal planning problem entirely.
5. **The tropical staples are all vegetatively propagated**, which closes the
   [seed-independence](seeds-and-propagation.md) loop completely:

| Crop | Propagation | Seed needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Taro (kalo) | Huli — cut tops | **No** |
| Sweet potato ('uala) | Slips from tubers | **No** |
| Cassava | Stem cuttings | **No** |
| Banana / plantain | Pups / suckers | **No** |
| Breadfruit ('ulu) | Root shoots | **No** |
| Sugarcane | Stem cuttings | **No** |

**Not one of those needs a seed packet.** Between taro, sweet potato, cassava,
banana, and breadfruit you have a genuinely calorie-complete staple set that
propagates itself indefinitely — and breadfruit in particular is one of the
highest-calorie-per-tree crops that exists.

That is a far stronger food-security position than anything the temperate
recommendations above can offer, and it makes the aquaponics system a
**complement** for fresh greens rather than the centrepiece.
