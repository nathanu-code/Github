## What it is

The same reverse osmosis principle as the hand pumps, with an electric or petrol
motor doing the pressurising instead of your arm. This removes the human
stamina ceiling entirely and moves the constraint to **power budget**.

This is the category for off-grid coastal homes, cruising yachts, and anyone who
needs water continuously rather than in a survival emergency.

## The real decision: power, not price

Output scales almost linearly with amps. A 65 L/hr unit pulling 24 A at 12 V is
roughly **290 W** while running. Before choosing capacity, check:

1. **Battery bank and charge source.** Can you sustain the draw, or will you run
   the watermaker only while the engine or genset is on?
2. **12 V vs 24 V.** The same unit halves its amp draw at 24 V. On a 24 V boat
   this materially changes the wiring and fusing.
3. **Duty cycle.** A 30 L/hr unit run 2 hrs/day beats a 65 L/hr unit you can
   only afford to run 20 minutes.
4. **Petrol option.** Rainman's petrol units sidestep the electrical budget
   entirely at the cost of carrying fuel.

## Where it fits vs. the manual devices

| | Manual RO | Electric watermaker |
|---|---|---|
| Output | 0.89–8 L/hr | 6–65+ L/hr |
| Constraint | Human stamina | Amp-hours / fuel |
| Fails when | You are injured or exhausted | Power dies |
| Role | Survival | Supply |

They are **not substitutes — they are layers.** An electric watermaker is your
water supply; a hand pump is what you grab when the boat's power is gone and
you are in the raft. A cruising boat wants both.

## Notes / due diligence

- Pre-filtration and pickling matter more than on manual units. An idle
  membrane fouls; commissioning and lay-up procedure drives real lifetime cost.
- Quoted L/hr assumes reasonable feedwater temperature and salinity. Cold or
  brackish water shifts output.
- Confirm current production status before quoting: several well-known models
  (PowerSurvivor 40E among them) are being discontinued or rebranded across the
  Katadyn/Spectra lines.
