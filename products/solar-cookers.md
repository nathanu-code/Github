## Verdict

**They work genuinely well in direct sun — and that qualifier is the whole
story.** A solar cooker is a real cooking appliance, not a novelty. It is also
the only cooking method on this list that fails for reasons entirely outside
your control.

Treat it as a **fuel extender and a water pasteuriser**, never as a primary
stove. Every meal it cooks is propane you didn't burn — that is real value in a
long emergency, and it doesn't change the
[propane conclusion](stoves-and-fuel.md).

## The four types

| Type | Temperature | Time to boil | Price | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Panel** (e.g. CooKit) | ~90–200 °C | 1–2 hrs | ~$30–50 | Cheapest, packs flat, weakest and most wind-sensitive |
| **Box** | 90–200 °C (194–392 °F) | 1–2 hrs | ~$100–300 | Best all-rounder: forgiving, big capacity, holds heat |
| **Evacuated tube** (GoSun) | up to ~290 °C (550 °F) | ~20 min for a meal | $45–249 | Fastest; vacuum insulation tolerates cool, windy, hazy conditions |
| **Parabolic** | **>300 °C (570 °F)** | **10–15 min** | Varies | Stove-equivalent — can actually fry and sear |

Box and panel cookers heat gradually and take 1–3 hours to cook most foods.
Parabolics concentrate light hard enough to behave like a conventional burner.

## How the real products perform

**GoSun Sport ($249)** — the standout on speed. Bakes, roasts, or steams a meal
for two in **about 20 minutes in direct sun**, which is competitive with a gas
stove. Its evacuated tube means it still functions in cool air and light wind,
where box cookers struggle.

**GoSun Go (~$45)** — the compact version. 20–30 minutes in direct sun.

**All American Sun Oven (~$287)** — top of the box-cooker price range and
generally considered worth it. Larger capacity than the tube cookers and better
suited to actual pots and baking dishes.

**Solavore Sport** — reviewers were less impressed: awkward reflector
attachment, and internal seams harder to clean than competitors.

## The honest limitations

### Overcast destroys performance

Marketing claims that these cook "in any weather" do not survive testing. On a
hot but **overcast** day, prawns in a GoSun took **2 hours 15 minutes** —
against roughly 20 minutes in direct sun. That's a 6–7× penalty, and the GoSun
is the most weather-tolerant design in the category.

Solar cookers run on **direct beam** sunlight. Diffuse light through cloud
carries a fraction of the usable energy. Cloud doesn't slow a solar cooker
down — it takes it out.

### No cooking after sunset

This sounds obvious and is routinely under-weighted. People cook dinner in the
evening. A solar cooker cannot. Practically, it means shifting your cooking to
midday and eating retained-heat food later — a genuine behavioural change, not
a spec footnote.

### Sun angle and season matter

Output depends on how directly the sun strikes the aperture. Low winter sun,
short days, and high latitudes all cut performance materially — exactly the
season when hot food matters most. If you're at northern latitude, do not plan
winter cooking around one.

### They need babysitting

Box and panel cookers want realignment as the sun moves. Parabolics — which
concentrate the hardest — need it most frequently, and their focal point is a
real **burn and eye hazard**. A parabolic is not a set-and-forget appliance and
is a poor choice around children.

### The failure mode is correlated with the emergency

This is the point that matters most for a preparedness kit. Storms, wildfire
smoke, dust, and ash all block direct sun — and those are frequently *the
emergency itself*. A solar cooker is most likely to be unavailable on precisely
the days you most need to cook.

That is why it's a layer, not a foundation.

## The best reason to own one: water pasteurisation

This is arguably better value than the cooking, and it ties directly into the
[rainwater](water-catchment.md) and [filtration](water-filtration-rain-and-salt.md)
records.

**You do not need to boil water to make it safe.** Holding water at **65 °C
(149 °F) for 6 minutes** kills all pathogens of drinking-water concern —
Hepatitis A, Giardia, Cryptosporidium, rotavirus, E. coli, Entamoeba, Shigella,
Salmonella, and the organisms behind cholera and typhoid.

65 °C is far below boiling and **well within reach of even a cheap panel
cooker.** That means:

- **Fuel-free disinfection** of rainwater or filtered surface water, in volume,
  with no consumables at all.
- A **WAPI** (Water Pasteurisation Indicator) — a small polycarbonate tube of
  wax that melts and drops when 65 °C is reached — confirms it worked. A few
  dollars. One study found the WAPI 100% effective at producing E. coli–negative
  results when used with a CooKit panel cooker.

A $40 panel cooker plus a WAPI is a serious water-treatment capability that
consumes nothing and cannot wear out. That is a stronger argument than any of
the cooking claims.

## Recommendation

**Buy one, size the expectation correctly.**

- **Best single pick:** an evacuated-tube cooker (GoSun Sport class, $249) if
  you want speed and the widest weather tolerance; an
  **All American Sun Oven (~$287)** if you want capacity, real pots, and baking.
- **Best value:** a **panel cooker (~$30–50) plus a WAPI.** If the primary job
  is fuel-free water pasteurisation and occasional slow cooking, this captures
  most of the benefit for under $60.
- **Parabolic:** only if you want genuine stove-equivalence and accept constant
  tracking plus a burn/eye hazard.
- **Skip** if you're at high latitude and planning for winter.

## Where it sits in the system

| Layer | Fails when |
|---|---|
| Propane, 20 lb tanks | Tanks run dry |
| Wood stove | (Effectively never) |
| **Solar cooker** | **Overcast, night, low sun angle** |
| Alcohol | Alcohol runs out |

Its failure mode is weather, which no other layer shares — so it adds real
independence. But because that failure correlates with the emergency itself, it
belongs **after** propane and wood in the [build order](preparedness-system.md),
not before.

## Sourcing note

**Tier 1 — safe to source generically.** A solar cooker has no pressure vessel,
no fuel, no lithium, and no electronics. The failure mode is "cooks slowly,"
not fire or poisoning. Reflective panel cookers in particular are trivial
commodity items and can even be built from cardboard and foil.

The one thing worth paying for is **glass and seal quality** on box cookers, and
**vacuum integrity** on evacuated tubes — a tube that loses its vacuum becomes
an ordinary, mediocre cooker. See [`sourcing-from-china.md`](sourcing-from-china.md).
